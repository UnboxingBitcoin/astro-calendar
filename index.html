Method, coverage and checks

Coverage

  • 12 Nov 2023 – 12 Nov 2029 (3 years either side of build date): every event. Sign ingresses for all bodies including Moon, Chiron, Rahu and Ketu in both zodiacs; retrograde and direct stations; the five major aspects (conjunction, sextile, square, trine, opposition) between Sun, Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune, Pluto and Chiron; new and full moons; eclipses; daily positions.
  • 1959 – 2050: eclipses, plus slow planets (Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune, Pluto, Chiron, Rahu, Ketu): ingresses in both zodiacs, stations, and major aspects between them.
  • 1500 – 2100: Saturn, Uranus, Neptune and Pluto: ingresses in both zodiacs, stations, and major aspects between them.
  • Moon aspects are left out on purpose: they add about a thousand entries a month.

How the data was made

All times come from Swiss Ephemeris 2.10 (Astrodienst), the engine behind astro.com and astro-seek. Sidereal uses the Lahiri ayanamsa (true, with nutation). Rahu and Ketu are the mean node, the default in Indian panchangs. Times are Indian time (UTC+5:30) throughout, rounded to the minute. Eclipse times are greatest eclipse; dates before 15 Oct 1582 are Julian calendar.

Checked against published tables

  • 412 / 412 eclipses 1959–2050 match NASA / Espenak catalogues for date and type, all within 2 minutes.
  • 37 / 37 tropical Saturn ingresses 1959–2000 match astro.com's published list to the minute.
  • 44 / 44 sidereal ingresses in 2025–27 (Sun, Venus, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn) match PanchangBodh's New Delhi tables within 1 minute; Saturn and Jupiter retrograde dates match too.
  • 16 / 16 2026 events (ingresses, stations, aspects, new moon) from Alabe's "Hot Degrees 2026" match within 1 minute.
  • Pluto and Neptune ingresses from the 1800s differ from astro.com's list by 4–11 minutes. That comes from a different JPL ephemeris version, and the gap grows further back. Read pre-1900 outer-planet times as accurate to about a quarter hour, and pre-1800 times more loosely.
  • Drik Panchang's Sankranti times run about 6 minutes later than standard Lahiri, so its sidereal times differ slightly from this page. For slow planets the gap can be an hour or more.

Sources used for checks: NASA Eclipse Web Site (eclipse.gsfc.nasa.gov), EclipseWise, astro.com Swiss Ephemeris ingress files, PanchangBodh, Alabe Hot Degrees 2026, Drik Panchang.
